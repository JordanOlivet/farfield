# Farfield 1.0.0

Minecraft 1.21.1, NeoForge and Fabric.

## Before you update

- **Update the server and every client together.** 1.0.0 cannot stream
  with 0.3.x (protocol 12, was 9): a mismatched client plays normally,
  without the distant terrain.
- **The first start rebuilds the preview.** The preview tiles of 0.3.x are
  discarded and built again in the new format, so the not-yet-generated
  horizon takes a while to fill the first time (on a server, the pregen
  within `preview_spawn_radius` runs again). The LODs of the terrain you
  explored are kept.

## Farther

- The distant terrain and the preview reach as far as you set, from 256
  blocks to the world border (Fog Distance, 4,096 by default), in sixteen
  levels of detail; under a shader pack up to 262,144 blocks.
- Servers send the LODs and the preview to each player's own distance,
  within `streaming_radius` (default 4,096, up to the border).
- `preview_spawn_radius` goes to the world border: every level within
  16,384 blocks of the spawn, and past that what a player at the spawn sees.

## Faster

- The preview is built as a pyramid, coarse first, nearest and in front
  first, so the horizon fills in seconds; the costliest and invisible
  features of a pack are left out by measure, not by list.
- A full performance pass with the image unchanged: about half the render
  time of a steady frame, a third less preview meshing, half the server's
  time per chunk, and a join that waits for nothing it does not need (the
  block looks from the cache in seconds, more sections asked at once).
- The view fills from you outward after a join or a teleport.

## Fixed

- Black areas at small render distances (4 chunks) along the bottom and
  sides of the view.
- The End's islands in the preview; the preview on Fabric in the End.

## Settings

- Single player: the distance within which the preview grows the world's
  own trees and plants (Preview tab).
- Thread counts go up to your computer's logical processors; Auto is one
  per three logical processors.
