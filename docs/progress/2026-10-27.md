## What was done

The user (2026-10-26): "Fais le point 1 et 4 maintenant. On fera la
release une fois que toutes les fonctionnalités actuelles seront
complètes", and "Ne t'occupe pas de voxy". Work started on 2026-10-26 in
the evening and was checked today. ADR-070.

- **Other protocol versions declined.** A server answers a hello of
  another version with a refusal: a welcome of session -1 carrying its
  own version (`Welcome.declined`); it reads only the hello's first field,
  so another hello shape does not matter. The client logs both versions
  and builds its own LODs. A client also builds its own LODs after 6
  hellos without an answer (60 s), for servers from before this change.
  Before, such a client waited forever and drew nothing.
- **The server's ids translated on receipt.** The mirror's key tables are
  its own again: `KeyTranslation` keeps the server's keys in id order
  beside the mirror (`server-block-states.txt`, `server-biomes.txt`) with
  the mirror's id of each. A writer thread (`farfield-stream-mirror`)
  decodes each received section, remaps its palette and biomes
  (`LodSection.remapIds`) and stores it (`LodWorld.putSection`), or stores
  the record as it came when no id changes; tiles likewise
  (`PreviewTile.remapIds`, `PreviewStore.decodeFile`). A section or tile
  the writer could not store is fetched again. The frozen tables are gone
  (`KeyRegistry.freeze`, `idForced`, `find`; `MaterialTable`'s fall-backs
  to the bare ground): the preview's covered materials get their key on a
  streamed client, so the speckle can come back on coarse levels. A
  mirror from before (tables equal to the server's, no key files) keeps
  its tables as the server's keys: no refetch.
- **Fix:** a tile's file could not replace the old one on Windows while a
  preview thread read it (`AccessDeniedException` on the move, seen once
  in the first run; the tile was fetched again). `PreviewStore` now tries
  the move again for a moment.
- Stat line: `translated` sections; the mirror's close line counts the
  server's keys and its own.
- Core tests: `KeyTranslation` (keys held again, a gap, a disagreement),
  a section translated into a mirror with a key of its own,
  `PreviewTile.remapIds`, `decodeFile` on bad files, the refusal's shape.

## The user's test on their server: holes and white flecks over new ocean

The user started their server (`docker compose ... up -d` in their stack
folder, which they named) and tested with the new jars: "premier test
concluant" (terrain appears while walking, without reconnecting, preview
off). Second test, preview on, teleported over new deep cold ocean
(39334, -857): black rectangles on the sea, and white flecks under water
that vanish when zooming. `/farfieldclient render off` removed the
rectangles (LOD geometry); `debug levels` showed them on level 0.

Read on copies (the user's mirror, one region copied from their server
store; deleted after): stored light right everywhere, no column ending
under sea level. Reproduced on the Fabric dev server and client over new
deep ocean (45064, -768, from `locate biome` far away): the same black
rectangles. A copy of the dev mirror against the dev server store: the
mirror's surface sections there held 512 or 768 water voxels of 1,024 at
y 62, the server's 1,024: they were fetched while one or two of their
chunks were still generating, and never again.

- **Fix 1, outdated sections fetched again** (`StreamClient.index`): the
  change notices made the client read the region's index again, but the
  renderer asks only for sections it lacks, so a drawn copy stayed. Now
  every section the mirror holds at another version than the index goes
  into the background queue; the stat line counts `outdated`. The black
  holes were a surface section missing a chunk the deep section under it
  had: the dark sea floor showed through.
- **Fix 2, border faces meshed again** (`BinaryGreedyMesher.borderFaces`,
  `LodRenderer.applyRewrites`): a face on a section border takes its
  light from the neighbor, or full light while the neighbor is missing;
  only sections with translucent faces were meshed again when a neighbor
  came, so a sea floor section whose top is its top layer kept full light
  (white patches with straight edges). The mesher now reports the
  borders whose light its faces read, and a written neighbor meshes
  those sections again.

Verified (Fabric dev server and client, vanilla renderer): the rejoin of
the mirror with holes fetched 364 outdated sections and the holes were
gone at the same view; a teleport into another new ocean while connected
refetched 241, 98, 22, 21 outdated sections over the next minute, no hole
in the capture; a third new ocean with fix 2: no hole, no straight-edged
pale patch. Mesh jobs over the fill: 5,765 against 5,881 before fix 2,
view settled at 19.7 s against 18.2 s. `gradlew build` passes, with a core
test for the border mask. The dev server was killed at the end (the stop
command could not be written): only the test world; its lock was removed.
