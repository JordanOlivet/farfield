## The worst frames (ADR-148)

The user asked why item 2 (a persistent mapped upload buffer) was left
out; measured first ("mesure 2"): uploads never behind (at most 64
meshes waiting, 2 to 3 ms from mesh to upload, the 3 ms budget cut 0 to 7
frames in 10 s), the quad copy 2 to 5 ns a quad. The worst frames came
from elsewhere: the selection's application (3 to 12 ms a selection in
flight), the preview swaps (300 to 600 meshes in one frame while loading),
the evictions (a walk over every resident, one buffer call per
neighbor-table int). The user: "go a b et c".

- (a) A swap's quads sent as its meshes arrive; a re-meshed section keeps
  its blend until the blends' budget reaches it.
- (b) The selection applied within 1 ms a frame into a second set of lists;
  the evictions over per-slot arrays; the neighbor tables' single ints in
  one compute pass a frame.
- (c) Not done: the 5 mesher threads are saturated (49.7 s of meshing in
  10 s, jobs waiting 21 to 66 ms), more in flight only lengthens the queue.

A first run drew holes everywhere: the build height the column tests read
was no longer set with the new lists; fixed before the A/B.

A/B against 8a94e22 in the same session (the GPU at about 6.9 ms a frame on
both sides, twice the earlier runs', standing still too: the PC's state):
render thread in flight 2.2-2.55 to 2.1-2.45 ms; the worst frame per 10 s
in flight 12-17 ms to 5.5-6.8 ms, except three windows with one quad copy
of 18 to 33 ms (the driver waiting); swap frames while loading 10-23 ms to
7.5-10.6 ms; captures 0.08 % and 0.29 % apart. Multiplayer: streaming, no
exception, 1.5 to 1.8 ms in flight.

Not verified: shader packs, the NeoForge pack, the user's own flight.
