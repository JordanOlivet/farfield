## The preview's speed against the SeedGen mods (no code)

The user shared Voxy-SeedGen (modrinth.com/mod/voxy-seedgen) and Distant
Horizons SeedGen (modrinth.com/mod/distant-horizons-seedgen), which fill
LODs from the seed far faster than the noise preview, and asked whether
Farfield took the problem the wrong way. Read from their Modrinth pages
only: both are All Rights Reserved with no public source, and their
numbers are their author's.

- Same approach as ours (ADR-037/038): terrain from the seed, outside the
  chunk system. Voxy-SeedGen: solo and LAN only, solid stone underground,
  "N-sized worldgen" (each area built at the detail the renderer draws).
  DH-SeedGen: trees faked, mods with their own generator unsupported.
- Per thread: Voxy-SeedGen claims 13.2 M chunks in 40 s on 28 threads,
  about 11,800 chunks/s. The preview: about 5,000 for the heights alone
  (ADR-058, about 200 ms a 1,024-chunk tile), 230 to 350 with the
  features (2.9 to 4.5 s a tile, 2026-11-08).
- The gap: the real features (ADR-079) run chunk by chunk at every
  distance, over 90 % of a tile (ADR-123); pregeneration builds the finest
  grid everywhere (ADR-123); `preview_threads` Auto is one per three
  logical processors, 1 to 6.
- Candidates, the first proposed: the real feature sandbox near (under 1
  to 2 km) and a per-biome canopy learned from sandbox-built tiles
  farther, measured on a far tile first; more threads for a server's
  pregeneration while nobody is online; trees faked everywhere (what
  ADR-079 dropped for modded trees). Noted in plan item E.2.
- Not worked on today: the user picked it as a topic for the next
  session.

## The preview built coarse to fine: study (branch `feat/preview-pyramid`, no code)

The user asked for a preview built in several steps, coarse levels first,
each drawn as soon as it exists, after a video of the SeedGen mods showing
the terrain to the world border right after a world's creation. Study in
`docs/analysis/preview-coarse-to-fine.md`: a pyramid of tiles (a level
covers twice the side with the same 129 x 129 heights), coarse first, a
terrain pass then a features pass per tile, the real feature sandbox near
and a canopy from a measured profile per biome farther.

The user's answers: real features to 1,250 blocks as a setting; explored
terrain far away shows its real LODs at coarser levels (the store's mip
pyramid grows above level 4), not the seed's terrain; a threads setting
for the pregeneration, default Auto; the world border is the final
target, planned for from the start.

Measure 2 of the study, on the NeoForge dev server (`/farfield spike tile
at`, four tiles 20,000 to 40,000 blocks out): of a 4.2 s tile, the surface
rules take about 1.8 s, making the stand-in chunks (two per chunk) about
0.9 s, the features themselves 0.5 to 0.9 s, the heights 0.4 to 0.6 s. The
ground costs four times the features in vanilla; a cheaper ground with
the same result became the study's step 2.

The same command by the user on the pack server (idle, one tile at
200,000, 200,000): 9.8 s, the mods' vegetation 4.8 s (half), the stand-in
chunks 2.1 s, the surface rules 1.6 s, the heights 0.7 s. Both levers
count there.

Measure 1, with a new spike command (`/farfield spike heights at <x> <z>`,
`NoisePreview.heightsSpike`, both loaders compile): a tile's 129 x 129
heights take 0.43 to 0.6 s at every spacing from 4 blocks to 131,072
(the border's level), searched from the neighbor's height; from the top
1.4 s. A coarse tile keeps today's search. The two searches disagree on
1 to 2.7 % of the samples, by up to 174 blocks, at every spacing, level 0
included. Looked into apart (the user: "tu peux creuser à part"): the
spike now also asks the generator's exact height (`getBaseHeight`) on the
differing samples. The neighbor search is the wrong one (mean error 11 to
35 blocks there, against 2.8 to 4.9 from the top), mostly too low: once a
sample falls into an open cave or under an overhang the next ones follow
it, as `surface` checks only 16 blocks above. About 1 to 2 % of today's
preview heights are tens of blocks off. Not fixed: in the study, with the
constraint (stay near the neighbor search's cost).

Measure 3, with a temporary core test (deleted) over the 232 tiles of the
dev world: a biome's cover tells biomes apart (plains 1.6 %, forests 50
to 55 %, jungle 80 %); at 16-block voxels the trees of ordinary forests
fill 0.2 to 2.8 % of the voxel columns, so today's far tiles pay for
features that are almost never drawn; at 8-block voxels about half.

The user's pack ran on the PC during the dev runs of measures 1 to 3:
their absolute times are to take again with the PC free.

## Step 2: cheaper stand-in chunks (ADR-125)

The user: "go étape 2". A JFR profile of six far tiles on the dev server
(attached with `jcmd` to the game JVM: `JAVA_TOOL_OPTIONS` also reaches
Gradle's JVM and broke its toolchain detection) put a third of a tile in
`SurfaceSystem.buildSurface` walking every block down to the world's
bottom, a tenth in priming heightmaps. The rules' stand-in now stops 64
blocks under its surface, holds one block in its unread columns, carries
no biomes and stands at the noise status; both stand-ins set their
heightmaps from the heights they were laid from.

- Measured (same six tiles and the spawn's 228 rebuilt from nothing, runs
  7 minutes apart): a far tile 3.1 to 2.2 s (-30 %), the stand-ins 1.9 to
  0.9 s, the rules 1.3 to 0.5 s; the spawn 2.5 to 1.6 min.
- Tiles compared with a temporary core probe (deleted): two builds of the
  committed code already differ on 41 of 234 tiles by a few feature
  columns (a race, not looked at). Against that, the new code changes only
  the cells of six eroded badlands tiles (2,413 cells, stone to
  terracotta): their pillars are now ruled as the game rules them.
- A first version left the rules' stand-in at the carvers status without
  priming its heightmaps after the rules: 65 tiles differed (swamp water,
  powder snow, badlands read under the blocks the rules set). The noise
  status fixed it; found by the same probe.
- `/farfield spike surface` over 10 biomes against really generated
  chunks: the ruled tops right as often as before, column for column
  (91.7 to 100 %).
- `./gradlew.bat build` passes. Not committed before this wrap-up, not
  pushed. Not measured in the user's pack.
