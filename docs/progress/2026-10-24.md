## Band at the vanilla edge over sea ice (ADR-055)

The user, Fabric without Sodium (Sodium had been in the client's mods
folder during the 2026-10-21 check): "les bandes apparaissent quand un
chunk passe de LOD à vanilla [...] une bande de 1 blocs qui restait en LOD
[...] les LOD ont une luminosité différente de vanilla". With
`/farfield debug levels` the band stayed uncolored ("c'est pas des LOD");
F3+Q (F3+A on his AZERTY layout) made it vanish.

- Ruled out first: vanilla compiling a chunk before its neighbors' light
  (it re-meshes on every light change, `setSectionDirtyWithNeighbors`),
  stored light (14 in ice and 13 under it, border columns as interior).
- Cause: LOD ice was one translucent layer, vanilla's two (ice top and the
  water top under it); rays crossing from the last vanilla column into LOD
  ground missed the water layer. The mesher now keeps the faces between
  fluid and other translucent voxels.
- That exposed dark one-block lines along LOD section borders: a neighbor
  section's water drawn after the ice. Translucent sections are now drawn
  far to near.

## Generation speed (ADR-056)

The user felt LOD building and the seed preview slower since 2026-10-20.

- JMH, run twice back to back (the first run of the session read +40 %
  on the preview mesher, the second flat): mesher before and after
  `7bcf7f6` the same within noise (`MeshBenchmark` 223/185/191 us against
  220/188/200; `PreviewBenchmark.mesh` level 2 204 against 207). Neither
  benchmark had a fluid, so the ice and water branch never ran:
  `PreviewBenchmark` gained a `frozen` case (sea under ice, water a
  fluid), 217 us at level 2 against 207 without ice, 210 at level 4 as
  without. Limiting the two layers to fine levels was dropped: the cost
  is small, and coarse levels can meet the vanilla edge at a low detail.
- Logs of the user's pack, same world: preview 42 to 50 tiles a minute
  (2.6 s each, 2 threads) on 2026-10-18, 182 then 76 (0.5 to 0.8 s, 4
  threads) today; mesher mostly `0 meshing`. Both faster, not slower.
- The user's idea, which the code confirmed: `LodSelector` walks the grid
  from one corner and the first 64 wanted sections were requested, the
  preview sorted by distance only. Now both nearest and in front first
  (`WantOrder`), the selection runs again when the view turns, the
  preview schedules every 10 ticks instead of 40. Two fill lines log
  the result.
- Fabric dev client, five runs with the world restored from one copy and
  a server-side teleport to 12000, 160, 8000 (236 tiles): tiles in front
  68.0 s before, 26.5 s after; all 68.0 s before, 34.0 s after; within
  1,024 blocks 16.0 s before, 20.0 s after. 32, 64, 96 and 128 meshes in
  flight gave the same times; it stays 64. The measurement switches
  (`farfield.rasterOrder`, `farfield.meshInFlight`, `farfield.benchTeleport`)
  are removed.
- Two earlier series were not valid: a static field read before its
  initializer crashed the client at start (fixed), then chat commands
  sent by keyboard did not reach the game, and two runner scripts left
  running by a stopped task started clients in parallel. The numbers
  above come from the clean series alone (one client per log).

| | Tile | Heights | Cells | Within 1,024 | In front | All |

byte-identical (`diff -r`). The temporary teleport switch is removed.

The user: "go optimisation des biomes de l'aperçu". One more Fabric dev
client run from the same world copy and teleport: the sea-level biome of
each quart is looked up once per tile (icebergs and frozen cells shared
it), and the climate sampler and `finalDensity` share one cache per
distinct marked function.

| | Tile | Heights | Cells | Within 1,024 | In front | All |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ADR-057 | 259 ms | 191 | 53 | 9.7 s | 14.7 s | 20.7 s |
| ADR-058 | 202 ms | 140 | 45 | 7.8 s | 12.8 s | 18.8 s |

2 of 558 tiles differ from the previous run by one frozen cell each, the
rest equal once decoded: vanilla's biome tree keeps the thread's last
result on an exact tie, so the lookup order breaks ties.
