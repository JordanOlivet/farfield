## The analysis method, as the user pointed out

The first pass over the user's recording looked for sky-coloured pixels
and frame-to-frame change spikes: it found the 2 to 3 frame hole at the
game's render edge (frames 336 to 338, 576 to 577) but none of the bands
nor the one-frame level-2 areas (frames 286, 294) the user then had to
annotate. Reusing it would miss them again. Better: a "flash" detector
(a zone that changes and comes back, frame N against N-1 and N+1),
validated first against the user's 18 annotated frames
(`<Videos>/farfield-frames-231234`, frames 1 to 350); and a detector in
the renderer that logs chunk columns whose drawn state (preview, real
level) changes and reverts within 3 frames.

## Agent playtesting, steps 3 and 4: flights and the frame probe (ADR-099)

The user, for this worktree: tools only, no debugging of the bugs on
`main` ("tu peux debug ce que tu rajoutes, juste on ne va pas debug les
soucis rencontrés sur main"). Step 5 is kept in mind, not done.

- Flights: `/farfieldclient record start|stop` and the inbox's `record`,
  `replay <name> [speed] [every]`; text files in `farfield/flights/`.
- Frame probe: `/farfieldclient debug frames` or the inbox's `frames on`;
  three GPU captures per frame, read back asynchronously, judged on a
  thread of its own by a flash detector, an erased-LOD check and a column
  flicker watch fed by the renderer's state; events and PNGs in
  `farfield/frames/<session>/`.
- `gradlew :core:flashScan` scores the flash detector on a cut recording.

Validation on the 2026-10-28 recording: the user's annotations are drawn
on the frames (18 frames, 13 glitches, found by their stroke colour). A
first pass "found" all of them, but through the strokes: with the strokes
taken out it found 3. The glitches are faint (a colour distance of about
26 of 765, the frames around them within 10); with 2x2 averaging and
finer thresholds: 10 of 13 found, 40 other frames flagged in 350, some of
them at the game's render edge, the rest not judged.

In game, both dev clients, fresh world, minimized: a 400-tick flight
replayed in 19.9 s and recorded back identical (once `record stop` takes
the sample of its own tick); the probe's first frame is right; 46 and 56
events in the flight, not judged. The probe costs frames: 1,169 to 1,548 fps without,
331 to 349 with it at 854x480 (about 2 ms a frame). Its own stat line
gives 0.4 ms a frame on the render thread once the analysis has its
thread and the buffers are recycled: the rest is on the GPU and driver
side of the three blits and readbacks.
